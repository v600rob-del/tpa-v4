How to use TPA

TPA · Trains, Planes and Automobiles · the UK's trains, buses and planes moving on one free map

TPA shows the UK's trains, buses and planes moving on one map, free, with no adverts and no sign-up. Here's how to get the most from it.

TPA works best on a computer, where there's room to see everything. Phones and tablets work well too, with a little less detail on screen, and we're making them even better.

What you're looking at

  • Buses are green square icons with their route number underneath.
  • Trains are purple round icons with their train number underneath, facing the way they're going.
  • Planes are drawn from above and turn to face their direction.
  • Traffic shows as coloured roads (green flowing, amber slow, red queuing), with signs for roadworks and incidents happening now.

Everything moves on its own. Positions come from official live data, and between reports TPA carries each vehicle along its real route so it moves smoothly.

Zooming in and out

  • Zoomed out, buses and trains are grouped into one bubble per town, such as "Leeds · 312 buses · 48 trains". Planes always show on their own.
  • Tap a bubble, or zoom in, and it splits into the individual buses and trains.
  • Railway stations and bus stations appear once you're zoomed in to town level.

Tapping a vehicle

Tap any bus, train or plane to open its box:

  • Buses: route, where it's going, next stop with its expected time, the stops it's calling at, the type of bus with a picture, its number plate and fleet number (where the company shares them).
  • Trains: where it's from and going to, whether it's on time, its calling points and the type of train.
  • Planes: the airline, flight number, aircraft type, height and speed, with more under "More details".

Follow me!

Press Follow me! in a vehicle's box and the map glides along with it. Press Stop following, drag the map, or close the box to stop.

Departure boards

Tap a railway station or bus station to see its live departures and arrivals, including platforms or stands and any delays. You can also find a station by name with the search box.

Bus stop boards are there too: switch on Bus stops in Tracking layers, zoom right in, and tap a stop.

Tracking layers

Open Tracking layers in the side menu to choose what you see. Planes, trains, railway stations, buses, bus stations and traffic start switched on. Bus stops start switched off to keep the map clear: tick them on if you want them, and your browser remembers your choice. Ships are coming soon.

Search

Type a place, station, bus station, flight, train or bus into Find a place. TPA understands the names people use, so "Hull Paragon" finds Hull station and "Kings Cross" finds King's Cross.

Questions people ask

Is this live?
As near as we can make it. Every vehicle comes from official live reports. Buses and trains don't report every second, so between reports TPA works out where each one is along its real route. The vehicle's box tells you when it last reported.
Why did a bus or train fade away?
It stopped sending reports for a while, often because it has finished its journey or gone back to the depot. Rather than leave it sitting there, TPA lets it fade away. It comes back as soon as it reports again.
Why can't I see individual buses when zoomed out?
There are over 20,000 buses on the road at busy times. Zoomed out, TPA groups them by town so the map stays clear and quick. Zoom in or tap a town bubble to see them.
Is it free?
Yes, always. No adverts, no accounts, nothing to install. If you enjoy it, you can buy Rob a coffee (or Geoff the cat some treats) from the Support TPA button.
Where does the information come from?
Buses from the Department for Transport's Bus Open Data Service (BODS), trains from Network Rail and National Rail Enquiries, planes from aircraft broadcasts, traffic from TomTom, and the map from OpenStreetMap. Full credits are under Data sources & credits in the map's menu.
Got an idea, or something looks wrong?
Use Got an idea? in the map's menu, or email enquiries@tpalive.co.uk. We read everything.
